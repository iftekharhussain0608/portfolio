Q&A

Questions I get asked most.

Straight answers on architecture, teams and AI, from delivery rather than slides.

What sets you apart as an architect?

I've sat on both sides of the table.

Most of my career has been spent designing and delivering Salesforce solutions. Now I work with enterprise customers on what happens after go-live, and that second view changed how I design. I still care about data models, limits and integration patterns, but I start from the question a customer will eventually ask: what did we actually get out of this? Architecture that can't answer that is only half done.

What breaks first when a Salesforce org starts to scale?

Rarely the feature. Usually the data and the limits.

Large data volumes expose everything: non-selective queries, triggers that were never bulkified, sharing recalculations nobody planned for, integrations that assume the happy path. I design for that up front, with selective queries, asynchronous processing where it belongs, and integration error handling that retries, logs and alerts instead of failing silently. Governor limits are cheaper to respect on the whiteboard than in production.

How do you lead engineering teams?

Clear direction, honest code review, and maintainable over merely shipped.

With teams of six to ten developers, the pattern that works is simple: agree the technical direction early, review code seriously, and invest in the things that make the next project faster. That's why I built a reusable LWC component library. Standard patterns meant less duplicated effort and fewer debates about how a component should behave. Mentoring is part of delivery, not a side activity.

Where does Agentforce actually pay off?

Building an agent is easy. Running one well in production is the real work.

The value shows up when agents are grounded in clean data, scoped to a clear job, and observable once they're live. Start with a narrow, high-volume use case, measure it against a business metric, and only then widen the scope. The skill that matters is shifting from assembling automations to monitoring and governing autonomous ones.

How do you close the gap between a feature and real business value?

Start from the outcome, not the release note.

Every engagement should begin with the business metric that's supposed to move: case resolution time, order accuracy, rep productivity. Then map platform capabilities to it, plan adoption as deliberately as the build, and keep measuring after go-live. A feature that's live but unused is cost, not value.

How do you use AI in your own work?

Hands-on, every day.

I build agentic workflows with Claude Code, custom skills and subagents to research, prototype and automate the repetitive parts of my work. Building them myself keeps me honest about what AI does well today, and where it still needs guardrails, review and a human in the loop. It's the same discipline I'd bring to an enterprise rollout.
